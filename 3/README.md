# R1 — THE SIGNAL

## What is signal?

The signal is everything that comes in. Sensory input. Raw data arriving at the body before the mind has a say.

The ears picking up frequencies — air pressure oscillations converted to electrical impulses by hair cells in the cochlea. The skin feeling pressure — mechanoreceptors firing in response to deformation. The nose that smelled something — volatile molecules binding to olfactory receptors, triggering pattern recognition older than language.

## How it works

R1 is the input ring. It sits between the ground (R0) and the gate (R2).

The ground gives you a body. The signal gives that body something to receive. The gate decides what gets through.

R1 processes raw input before the gate decides what matters. This is not passive reception. The sensory systems are active filters:

- **Ears**: The stapedius muscle modulates what frequencies reach the inner ear. The olivocochlear bundle sends signals *back* from the brain to the cochlea, tuning sensitivity in real time.
- **Skin**: Rapidly adapting receptors ignore constant pressure and fire only on change. Your nervous system is built to detect *difference*, not state.
- **Nose**: Olfactory habituation happens in minutes, but the initial signal has already been routed to the amygdala — the fastest sensory-to-emotion pathway in the body.

## Why p=3

3 is the first odd prime. The triangle. The minimum points to define a plane of perception.

Every signal requires three things: a source, a medium, and a receiver. Without all three, nothing comes in.
