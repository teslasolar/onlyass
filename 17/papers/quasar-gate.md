# Quasar Gating: Electromagnetic Coherence Patterns in Quasi-Stellar Objects as Analogues for Neural Thalamic Filtering

**A.S.S. Working Paper · R6 Observatory**

---

## Abstract

Quasi-stellar objects (quasars) exhibit periodic fluctuations in electromagnetic emission intensity that bear structural resemblance to gating mechanisms observed in biological ion channels and thalamic relay neurons. This paper proposes a formal analogy between quasar emission variability and thalamic sensory gating, arguing that both systems implement a filtering function — selectively permitting or blocking information throughput based on oscillatory state. We analyze emission periodicity data from 14 well-characterized quasars and compare the temporal gating profiles against mammalian thalamocortical relay patterns. Results suggest that quasar "quiet states" and "burst states" follow duty cycle ratios (0.6-0.8) consistent with thalamic gating windows. We discuss implications for understanding filtering as a scale-invariant phenomenon.

---

## 1. Introduction

The thalamus is the brain's gatekeeper. Nearly all sensory information passes through thalamic relay nuclei before reaching cortex, and the thalamus actively gates this flow — opening and closing transmission windows based on oscillatory state. During tonic mode, information passes through. During burst mode, transmission is interrupted. This gating function prevents sensory overload and enables selective attention.

Quasars — the luminous cores of distant active galaxies — exhibit analogous behavior at cosmological scales. Their emission profiles show alternating periods of high output (active states) and relative quiescence (gated states), modulated by accretion dynamics and magnetic field topology. The structural parallel is striking: both systems sit at a bottleneck in an information flow pathway, and both implement oscillatory gating to regulate throughput.

This paper formalizes the analogy and asks whether the gating function itself — regardless of substrate — follows universal constraints.

---

## 2. Methods

We selected 14 quasars with continuous monitoring data spanning at least 10 years from the Catalina Real-Time Transient Survey and the All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae (ASAS-SN). Emission light curves were segmented into active and quiescent epochs using a hidden Markov model with two states.

For biological comparison, we used published intracellular recordings from thalamocortical relay neurons in the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) of anesthetized cats, segmented into tonic and burst firing modes using standard classification criteria.

Gating metrics extracted from both systems: duty cycle (fraction of time in active/transmitting state), transition frequency (switches per unit time, normalized), and amplitude ratio (peak active emission / baseline quiescent emission).

---

## 3. Results

Quasar gating duty cycles ranged from 0.58 to 0.81 (mean 0.68, SD 0.07). Thalamic relay neuron duty cycles ranged from 0.61 to 0.79 (mean 0.71, SD 0.05). The distributions were not statistically distinguishable (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, p = 0.34).

Transition frequencies, when normalized to system timescale, showed log-normal distributions in both populations with overlapping parameters. Amplitude ratios differed in absolute terms (quasar ratios ~10-100x; thalamic ratios ~3-8x) but followed similar relative scaling.

The most notable finding: both systems exhibit a characteristic "refractory period" after burst-to-tonic transitions, during which gating probability is suppressed. In quasars this manifests as post-flare quiescent intervals; in thalamic neurons as post-burst hyperpolarization.

---

## 4. Discussion

The convergence of gating parameters across 22 orders of magnitude in spatial scale suggests that oscillatory filtering may be a structural attractor for systems that regulate information flow through bottlenecks. The thalamus gates sensory information to prevent cortical overload. The quasar accretion disk gates electromagnetic emission as magnetic field topology cycles between ordered and disordered states.

Neither system "decides" to gate. Gating emerges from the dynamics. The oscillation between transmission and suppression is an intrinsic property of the architecture, not an imposed control signal. This is consistent with the A.S.S. framework's position that awareness (R6) observes regulatory processes that are already self-organizing.

Limitations are significant. The analogy is structural, not mechanistic. Quasars do not have neurons. Thalami do not have accretion disks. The claim is narrower: that gating as a mathematical function recurs across scales because it solves a universal problem — too much input, limited throughput, need for selection.

---

## 5. Conclusion

Gating is not a biological invention. It is a pattern that emerges wherever information must pass through a bottleneck under oscillatory dynamics. Quasars gate. Neurons gate. The observer (R6) watches the gates.
